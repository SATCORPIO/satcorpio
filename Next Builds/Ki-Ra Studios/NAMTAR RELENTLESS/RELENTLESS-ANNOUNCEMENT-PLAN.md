## 0. Two judgments that shape everything below

### 0.1 This is an announcement, not a launch

Global launch is ~Q2 2029. Soft launch is ~Sep 2028. The build is in **Phase A
"Landfall", sprint 1**   a sector clock, one persisted outpost, a production
claim, and a Train queue. There is no art, no trailer, no screenshot, and no
date worth printing.

So the page cannot sell a product. What it can do is the four things that
actually compound over a 24-month build:

| Job | Why it is worth a page now |
|---|---|
| **Plant the differentiator publicly, and dated** | The Relentless Cycle is the one asset an incumbent cannot copy in a quarter (doc 21 §1.3). Being first and on the record is free today and expensive to buy later |
| **Start the owned audience** | Discord + a forwarding list. Doc 21 §6.1 counts owned channels as retention, not acquisition   but the list started in 2026 is the list the game soft-launches to in 2028 |
| **Credibility with press, creators and talent** | Doc 21 §9: *press and creators before paid.* A creator explaining the Cycle is credibility paid media cannot buy |
| **Recruiting** | The Roadmap needs 20–30 people. A page that states the pillars honestly is the cheapest hiring asset the studio will ever produce |

Nothing on the page should be measured in pageviews. It is measured in
forwarding addresses, Discord joins, and inbound from people who read it and
wanted to work on it.

### 0.2 The title is not cleared, and the page is external use

Doc 23 §2.1 and gate **L1** are explicit: trademark clearance search in Phase 1,
filings **before any public external use   first store listing, first press,
first UA creative**   and the working rule until clearance returns is *"internal
until counsel says otherwise."* §11 names this as the gate people breach by
accident, "usually by putting the title on a slide, a job ad, or a social
handle." A page on satcorp.io is that breach.

That is a business decision, not a build blocker, and the studio may
legitimately decide the announcement is worth going ahead of the search. Two
things make it cheap to be wrong:

1. **Run the clearance search now.** It is a search, not a filing, and it is the
   least expensive item in the entire dossier. Capture the domain and handles at
   the same time (doc 23 §2.3)   before the announcement, not after it.
2. **Build the page so a rename is one string edit.** Every appearance of the
   title resolves from a single exported constant (§3.4). This mirrors the
   naming law that already protects the game's own data (doc 05 §3.1): a name
   that has to change is a string-table edit, not a migration.

Everything below assumes the studio proceeds. The four **faction names** are a
separate call and the recommendation there is different   see §1.3.

# PART I   What should be announced
*Senior mobile game developer's read*

## 1.1 The three things that are true, provable, and worth saying

An announcement made from a Phase A build has exactly one currency: **being
specific about things that are already decided.** Feature lists are cheap and
every competitor has a longer one. Decisions are not.

### ① The game, stated plainly

> A **server-authoritative live-service mobile 4X survival strategy game**, set
> on the same planet as NAMTAR, built for phones in Unity 6 LTS. Combat
> auto-resolves on the server; the client never decides an outcome that matters.

Say "mobile 4X survival strategy" in the first sentence. The genre veteran in
the primary audience (25–45, doc 01 §8) knows exactly what that means and will
self-select in three seconds. Being coy about the category to seem fresh reads
as evasion to the only people who matter.

### ② The differentiator   this is the announcement

> *Every sector runs a planetary escalation clock. On a fixed, telegraphed
> cadence the world Surges against every outpost at once, and how the sector
> collectively survives it permanently changes the map everyone plays on.*

This is the whole reason to publish. Doc 01 §4.1 reason 1 is that it is visible
in fifteen seconds   which is what makes it a *page*, not a paragraph. The
page's job is to make a reader who has played four of these games understand,
in one screen, that the map is a simulation with an agenda rather than
furniture.

The fiction that carries it (doc 12 §2.1) is better than the mechanic alone and
should be on the page in two sentences: the relic network is a **maintenance
system running on a dead world**, and human settlement registers as a fault
condition. Not a villain. Not an angry planet. A machine repairing damage.

### ③ The pillars, published as commitments

The five pillars (doc 01 §5) and the anti-goals (§6) are the strongest
announcement material in the dossier, because they are the only content that is
simultaneously (a) already decided, (b) unlike the competition, and (c) costs
nothing to prove today. They read as a covenant rather than a feature list, and
a covenant is what the "seen every trope and is bored, not naive" audience is
actually shopping for.

Publish, in the page's own voice:

- **Progress is slowed, never erased.** Wounded, not dead. A player who loses
  badly can see the way back inside one session.
- **Every player matters to someone.** A free player who holds a wall produces
  value the whole sector can see. Nobody here exists to be farmed.
- **Strategy happens before the fight.** Composition, tech, intel, timing,
  loadout   not reflexes. A loss you cannot read is a bug.
- **Three minutes is an honest session.** Deeper if you have the evening. Never
  required.
- **The planet is the antagonist.** Other players are just the ones standing in
  the way.

And the anti-goals a reader can hold the studio to:

- No pay-to-win power store   money buys time and chances, never power
  unreachable by playing (doc 03 §6).
- No fake-gameplay advertising, ever (doc 21 §4.2).
- No home screen with forty things blinking on it.
- No wiki required   the level tables ship in the client.
- 40fps on a mid-tier Android phone outranks any visual feature.

**One caution, and it is real:** anything published here becomes a commitment
the studio is held to for three years and quoted back on launch day. Publish
only the promises that survive Phase A regardless of what the prototype
teaches. The five pillars and the anti-goals qualify; the Surge *cadence*
(weekly vs. sector-age, doc 01 §10 q1) does not   say "on a clock everyone can
see", never "every Sunday".

## 1.2 The line that must be drawn: two objects, one world

This is the most likely way the announcement goes wrong. The site already has a
NAMTAR page, a NAMTAR feature presentation on `/kira`, and a NAMTAR safe-house
cluster. Adding a second thing called NAMTAR without an explicit disambiguation
produces three bad outcomes at once: the flagship's reveal gets diluted, mobile
readers think the PC game is a mobile game, and PC readers think the studio
pivoted to mobile.

Handle it head-on with a comparison the reader cannot misread:

| | **NAMTAR** | **NAMTAR RELENTLESS** |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Open-world survival | 4X survival strategy |
| Where | PC & console | Phone |
| You are | One survivor on a planet | One outpost in a sector |
| A session | An evening | Three minutes, or an evening |
| Standing | In development | Phase A prototype |

Then one line to bind them: **the world is shared; the games are not.** Same
planet, same canon, same relic strata   two different pictures shot on the same
location. This is a strength, and stating it converts a confusion risk into
world-building.

## 1.3 What must NOT be announced

| Do not publish | Why |
|---|---|
| **Any date**   launch, soft launch, beta, "2028" | The Roadmap treats every date past Phase 1 as provisional and re-baselines twice. A date printed in 2026 is a broken promise in 2028 |
| **Monetisation specifics**   prices, pack ladder, banner rates, gacha odds | Every number in doc 17 is ⟨P⟩. Publishing a price that will change is worse than publishing nothing. The *philosophy* (no power for sale) is safe; the mechanics are not |
| **Soft-launch markets** | A Phase 4 decision (doc 21 §11 q1), and naming the Netherlands invites a randomised-mechanics question that cannot yet be answered (doc 07 §3) |
| **The four faction proper names** | Doc 23 §2.2: highest trademark exposure precisely *because* they appear in marketing, cleared Phase 1 month 3. Publish the four **roles**   Logistics, Bastion, Warfare, Technology   with their silhouettes and materials, and hold the names. On this site that is not a compromise; it is the house style (§3.3) |
| **Officer names, unit ladders, region names** | Same batch-clearance rule, and none of it helps the pitch |
| **Team size, funding, hiring numbers** | Invites "is this real?" scrutiny the announcement does not need to survive |
| **A pre-registration button** | Doc 21 §5 puts pre-registration at *global launch only*. A pre-reg list that ages two years is a dead list and a store-relationship problem. Collect a forwarding address instead |
| **Screenshots or a trailer** | There are none. Faking one contradicts doc 21 §4.2 on the very day the studio announces it will never do that |

## 1.4 What the page can honestly say about status

The dossier's own tone rule applies to the studio as much as to the fiction:
*understated, competent, specific.* The truthful ladder, no dates:

| Stage | Standing |
|---|---|
| Pre-production dossier | Complete   25 documents |
| Engine specification | Complete |
| Phase A "Landfall" prototype | In progress |
| Vertical slice | Scheduled |
| Soft launch | Not scheduled |
| Launch | Not scheduled |

"Not scheduled" is a stronger sentence than a fake date and it is the only one
that will still be true in eighteen months. On this site it renders as
`[REDACTED]`, which is both honest and on-theme.

# PART II   How to announce it, and what to say
*Senior game advertiser's read*

## 2.1 The strategy in one line

**Announce a design position, not a product.** The studio has no build to show
and no date to give, but it has a genuinely contrarian thesis about a
multi-billion-dollar genre   and a thesis is the one thing that travels for
free.

The corollary: **spend nothing on media.** Doc 21 §3 makes UA spend contingent
on retention bands that are two years away, and paid media pointed at a page
with no install button is a donation. Beat 1 is owned and earned only.

## 2.2 The hook, in priority order

Everything leads with the Cycle (doc 21 §4.1). Not the base, not the units, not
the officers   **the world with a clock.**

Order of arguments on the page, and in any conversation with press:

1. **The world escalates against you on a schedule, forever.**
2. **The map remembers what the sector held, and what it lost.**
3. **Which is why the person next to you matters, even if they never spend a
   cent.**

That third beat is the emotional pitch nobody else in the category can make
(doc 21 §4.1, "Held the wall"), and it is the one that converts a bored genre
veteran into someone who tells a friend.

**Positioning line, external (doc 01 §8):**
> *A 4X survival war game where the planet fights back   and the map remembers
> who held it.*

**Headline recommendation for the page**, in the Ki-Ra screening-room voice:
> **The second feature.**
> *A planet that repairs itself, and considers you the damage.*

## 2.3 Tone, and the one thing the copy must not do

Doc 12 §8 governs: dangerous not hopeless, understated not theatrical, specific
and physical   cold, dust, cable, rust. Survivors are tired, not edgy.

The failure mode for an announcement written in an excited voice is that it
promises scale ("a living world!", "endless war!") and therefore reads as every
other announcement in the category. The dossier's own tone is the
differentiator's best delivery system: state the mechanic flatly and let the
implication land. *"The reading rises. On a clock everyone can see, something
comes to correct it."* is more menacing than any adjective available.

**Ki-Ra's page voice adds a second register on top**   the fixer presenting a
destination. That is already the site's idiom and it fits a game about holding
ground with strangers unusually well.

## 2.4 The sequence

| Beat | When | Channel | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Clearance** | Before anything | Counsel | Title search + domain/handle capture (doc 23 §2.3) |
| **1. The page** | Launch day | `satcorp.io/kira` | This plan, §3 |
| **2. The room** | Same day | Discord | A dedicated channel; the page's CTA points here |
| **3. Field notes** | +1 week, then monthly | Forwarding list + Discord | Short dev-log posts. Two a month, indefinitely. The cadence *is* the credibility |
| **4. Creators & press** | +2–4 weeks, once the page has held up | Direct outreach | Genre creators first, trade press second. The pitch is the Cycle in 90 seconds, with the disambiguation from §1.2 |
| **5. Everything else** | 2028 |   | Store assets, pre-registration, paid media. Doc 21 §§5–6. Not this year, not next |

Beat 3 is the one that gets dropped and it is the one that matters. An
announcement with no follow-through reads as vapour by month four. If the studio
cannot commit to a monthly note, publish the page **without** a forwarding list
and keep only Discord   an unfed list is worse than no list.

## 2.5 What success looks like

| Measure | Not this |
|---|---|
| Forwarding addresses left | Pageviews |
| Discord joins still active at day 30 | Discord joins |
| Creators and press who can restate the Cycle correctly | Impressions |
| Inbound from people who want to work on it | Social reach |
| Zero corrections issued | Engagement |

That last one is the quiet one. The page will be quoted back in 2029. Every
sentence should be one the studio still wants attributed to it then.

## 2.6 The advertiser's own anti-goal, published

Doc 21 §4.2's creative policy   no fake gameplay, no fabricated reviews, no
gambling language, no faction coding to real-world identity   is normally an
internal document. **Put the first item on the page.** In a category where
misleading creative is the norm, announcing "we will never advertise gameplay
that isn't in the game" on day one is the cheapest, most differentiating and
most verifiable promise available, and it costs the studio nothing it was not
already giving up.

# PART III   Implementation
*Senior web designer & developer's read*

## 3.0 The placement decision

Three options were considered:

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A seventh establishment page (`/relentless`) | **Rejected.** Breaks the six-establishment doctrine (BUILD-PLAN §1), the FileTabs nav, and `lib/divisions.ts`. The game is a title, not a division |
| A dedicated sub-route (`/kira/relentless`) | **Later.** The right destination once real art, a trailer and a faction gallery exist. Premature now   it would be a route with three placeholder frames on it |
| **A section on `/kira`** | **Ship this.** The page is already a programme of works, and a screening room is exactly where a second picture gets announced |

**Slot: immediately after the NAMTAR "Feature presentation" section, before "The
safe houses."** The metaphor does the work   *feature presentation*, then *the
second feature*. NAMTAR keeps its primacy as the main event; the mobile game
gets real weight without stealing the flagship's frame. The existing "Not yet
issued" section below acquires a new meaning for free: one canister has come off
the shelf, and its closing line   *"The studio is busier than this page
suggests. That is deliberate."*   now reads as a promise being kept rather than
a hedge. **No change needed there.**

## 3.1 Theme fidelity   the non-negotiables

The brief says keep the current Ki-Ra theme. Concretely:

- **Accent stays `#1f6f6b`.** No second accent, and specifically not NAMTAR's
  `#d97e2f`   the mobile game is Ki-Ra's programme, presented in Ki-Ra's room.
  If a reader needs a colour to tell the two games apart, the copy has failed.
- **No new 3D.** The Projection Room owns the GPU on this route and the
  performance budget (BUILD-PLAN §11) is enforced. The one bespoke visual (§3.5)
  is SVG + CSS only.
- **Reuse the existing vocabulary**, do not invent: `WorldDossier`,
  `ProgrammeLine`, `Placeholder`, `Stamp`, `Reveal`, `ThreadLink`, `Redact`, and
  the `.label` / `.label-accent` / `.dossier` / `.rule` classes.
- **Placeholder discipline (BUILD-PLAN §12).** Every asset slot ships as a
  designed classified frame with a stamp and a file number. New range: `NR-0xx`.
- **Responsive note (BUILD-PLAN §7).** Placeholder grids stay multi-column at
  every width   stacked full-bleed, three empty frames are three screens of
  nothing to scroll past.
- **Reduced-motion parity**, zero exceptions (globals.css).
- **Type scale unchanged:** display serif for titles, mono in the margins, sans
  for body.

## 3.3 Where the legal constraint becomes the art direction

The faction names are held until clearance (§1.3). On this site that is not a
compromise   the page already ships a `Redact` component, a `[REDACTED]`
vocabulary, and `SEALED` stamps as its native language. Four factions presented
as **role, silhouette and insignia, with the name redacted**, is more compelling
than four names would have been, and it converts a legal gate into the single
most on-brand moment in the section.

Render each as: role (`LOGISTICS`), one line of character, and a redacted name.
The `Redact` component reveals real text on hover and keeps it in the DOM for
crawlers   that behaviour is correct everywhere else on the site and **wrong
here**. Use a plain bar with no underlying string, or pass a placeholder. A
redaction that leaks the thing counsel asked to be held is worse than no
redaction at all.

Recommended for **phase 2**, not the first ship   it is the best block in the
section and it deserves the insignia marks that do not exist yet.

## 3.5 The one bespoke component: `CycleClock`

The differentiator has to be legible in fifteen seconds (doc 21 §4.1) and prose
alone will not do it. One small visual, and it is the only new motion on the
page.

- **Form:** a horizontal escalation strip, not a dial. A baseline that climbs
  left to right, four evenly spaced ticks, and at each tick a `SURGE` mark where
  the line drops slightly and resumes higher than before. The rising sawtooth
  *is* the thesis: the world escalates with you, forever.
- **Implementation:** inline SVG, `stroke="currentColor"` inheriting
  `text-accent`, path drawn with `stroke-dasharray` animated once on scroll-in
  via the existing `Reveal` + GSAP setup. No canvas, no three.js, no library.
- **Reduced motion:** renders complete and static. Content parity, no exception.
- **Accessibility:** `role="img"` with an `aria-label` stating the mechanic in
  one sentence; the same sentence exists as visible caption text, so the visual
  is decoration over prose rather than the only carrier of meaning.
- **Precedent:** `components/worlds/pulse/heartbeat.ts` measures its chart path
  rather than guessing it (commit `a2bc8aa`). Same discipline here   derive the
  path from a small array of points, do not hand-tune a `d` string.

## Deviations from the plan, and why

1. **Phase 4 was built now, not deferred   and it is a brief, not a gallery.**
   The plan deferred `/kira/relentless` because a route carrying three empty
   frames is not worth a URL. That reasoning survives; the route avoids it by
   carrying what the section has no room for and art cannot supply: the Cycle in
   full, the covenant, the four factions, the three hostile families, the
   apparatus, and the form. It is worth reading before a single asset exists.

2. **`Comparison` and `Standing` were extracted as shared units.** Both would
   otherwise have been written three times   section, brief, and the existing
   provenance strip. The existing strip was moved onto `Standing` in the same
   pass: leaving a third copy behind, having just created the other two, is the
   worse outcome.

3. **The `/kira` section's second CTA is Discord, not the forwarding list.**
   Lenis owns scroll on this site, so a cross-route `#field-notes` anchor lands
   where it feels like landing. The form is offered on the brief, beside the
   thing it is for.

4. **The withheld faction names are a plain bar, not the `Redact` component.**
   `Redact` keeps the real words in the DOM and reveals them on hover   correct
   everywhere else on the site, and precisely wrong for text counsel has asked
   to be held.

5. **`Comparison` renders twice, and only one is ever in the accessibility
   tree.** Below `sm` the table would have put the second column off the edge of
   a phone behind a scrollbar. This is the one block that cannot afford to be
   half-read (§1.2), so on small screens it becomes a stack of pairs.

6. **`NOTIFY_WEBHOOK_URL=` was added empty to `.env.local`.** The action falls
   back to `DISCORD_WEBHOOK_URL`, so without this a local test posts into the
   live ops channel. Present-but-empty stops the `??` fallback   the same trick
   `PARTNER_WEBHOOK_URL` already documents in that file.

7. **The comparison rows were rewritten on rebase.** This work was built on a
   base six commits behind `origin/main`, and the incoming commits re-pitched
   the flagship   post-apocalyptic, Unreal Engine 5, and an **iOS/Android
   companion app**. That last one matters: without a row drawing the line, a
   reader meets two Ki-Ra things on a phone and merges them, which is the exact
   misreading §1.2 exists to prevent. Every row now quotes the flagship the way
   its own dossier one section above quotes it. **If that dossier is edited,
   edit `COMPARISON` with it.**
