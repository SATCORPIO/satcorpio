## 0. Three judgments that shape everything below

### 0.1 This is an advertisement for a product that does not exist yet

FORFEITURE is in pre-production. Design documents and vertical-slice planning
are complete; there is no build, no footage, no render, and no date. The
marketing brief is unusually clear-eyed about this and its "what not to
advertise" section is longer than its "what to advertise" section. That is the
right instinct and this plan does not soften it.

So the page cannot sell a product. What it sells is **a position**, and the
position is unusually defensible:

| Job | Why it is worth a page today |
|---|---|
| **Plant the moat publicly, and dated** | *Law enforcement is a real, systemic, playable profession with its own progression.* Nobody else in this genre can say it. Being first and on the record is free now and unbuyable later |
| **Convert the two adjacent audiences** | FiveM/GTA-RP and Rust players already want this and are already unhappy with what they have. They are the cheapest conversion in the category and they read pages like this one |
| **Recruit** | A persistent-sim crime MMO needs specific, senior people. An honest page about the systems is the cheapest hiring asset the studio will produce |
| **Start the owned list** | The forwarding address collected in 2026 is the list Early Access launches to. There is nothing else to collect, because there is nothing to pre-order |

Nothing here is measured in pageviews. It is measured in forwarding addresses,
Discord joins, and inbound from people who read it and wanted to build it.

### 0.2 The brief's hardest constraint is also the art direction

The brief bans, on sight: neon noir, cyberpunk, saturated crime glamour, lens
flares, "cool gangster" visual language, and anything that makes crime look
aspirational. Its test is *if an image would look at home on an album cover, it
is wrong; if it looks like a photograph taken from a car window, it is right.*

This is not a problem for satcorp.io. It is the single closest fit between a
game's art bible and this site's existing fingerprints that the studio has had.
The site is already black, bone and red; already sets status as a rubber stamp
rather than a coloured pill; already runs 35mm grain and a vignette over
everything; and already ships every empty asset slot as a designed classified
frame with a file number rather than a "coming soon" tile. A dossier is what a
case file looks like, and FORFEITURE is a game about case files.

**The one place the two systems must be reconciled is colour**   see §3.1.

### 0.3 The name is cleared. The claims are not

Unlike NAMTAR RELENTLESS, there is no trademark gate here. `NAMING.md` §2
records a completed US check on 15 September 2026: Steam clear, no live USPTO
mark in Class 009 or 041, domains available. The title can be published.

What is *not* cleared is the feature set. Every sentence on this page becomes a
commitment the studio is held to and quoted back on Early Access day, two or
more years from now. The discipline that follows is the same one the sibling
plan used and it is the reason §3.4 puts every string in one reviewable module:
**publish decisions, never features.** A decision already taken internally costs
nothing to publish and is the only currency a pre-production page has. A feature
list is a promise the first build will break.

# PART I   What gets advertised

## 1.1 The hierarchy, in order

The brief gives four "things worth advertising" and a five-row audience table.
Ranked for the page, with the ranking being the actual content:

**① The moat, stated as a fact rather than a boast.**

> Law enforcement is a profession here, with its own progression, played by
> other people.

This is the whole advertisement. Every incumbent in the persistent-crime space
runs its consequence layer on people   moderators, whitelists, applications,
appeal threads. FORFEITURE runs it on systems. The crime-roleplay audience has
spent years being adjudicated by volunteers and knows exactly what that
sentence promises. It is also the only claim in the brief that is
simultaneously (a) already decided, (b) impossible for the incumbents to copy
without rebuilding, and (c) provable today, because it is an architecture
decision rather than a feature.

**The brief's own slogan for this was cut**, and rightly. It offers *"the rules
are in the code, not in the Discord"*, which fails the brief's own voice test
two paragraphs later: headlines read like a case file or a business plan, never
a movie poster, and that is a movie poster. A slogan also cannot be held to
anything. The replacement is duller on the page and stronger in an argument,
which is the correct trade for a studio asking to be believed about a game
nobody can play yet.

**② The fantasy, stated as the thing it is *not*.**

> Not "be a gangster." That fantasy is already well served, by studios with far
> bigger budgets than ours, and we would lose.
> **Operational competence under pressure** — the small-business owner whose
> business happens to be illegal.

Leading with a rejection is unusual and correct here. The audience the page
wants has been sold the gangster fantasy repeatedly and is bored of it; the
audience the page does *not* want self-selects out in one sentence, which is
worth more than the traffic it costs.

**③ Evidence that physically exists.**

The single most legible system in the game and the one that makes the police
side a profession rather than a chore. A shell casing exists in the world. It
gets bagged, logged, and the chain of custody can break. A criminal can sweep a
scene before the first unit arrives.

This is the section that earns the page's one bespoke visual (§3.5). Two
quantities behave differently   what the city suspects decays on its own, what
a detective has physically recovered rises in steps and can be lost   and that
difference is the entire detective loop. It is also the reason this argument is
made by a **drawing** and not by a sentence. The build tried both; the sentence
was cut for sounding like one. See the development record, entry 5.

**④ The four pillars**, published as commitments rather than features: the city
runs while you are logged off · evidence exists in the world and has to be
collected · there are no wipes and no seasons · losing costs you property and at
most forty-five minutes.

Each is a flat statement of what a system does, and that is a rule rather than a
preference   see the development record, entry 6.

**⑤ The Chronicle.** The no-wipe promise made into an artefact. The brief is
right that this is the best "come see what our players built" hook the site will
ever have   and right that it can only half-ship today, because there is no
shard with history yet. The promise is postable now; the rendered page is not.

## 1.2 The line that must be drawn: this is not the third NAMTAR

The sibling plan spent a whole section preventing readers from thinking the
flagship went mobile. FORFEITURE's version of that risk is different and
smaller, but it exists: the studio now has three announced titles, two of which
share a universe and one of which does not. A reader arriving at `/kira` meets
NAMTAR, NAMTAR RELENTLESS and FORFEITURE within two screens of each other.

The disambiguation is cheap because the categories are genuinely unlike:

| | **NAMTAR / RELENTLESS** | **FORFEITURE** |
|---|---|---|
| World | A dead super-Earth | A city, this century |
| The antagonist | The planet | The evidence you left |
| The other players | Rivals on a map | Rivals, and the detective working your case |

No comparison table is needed on the page for this   the accent colour, the
copy voice and the establishment framing do it. What *is* needed is that
FORFEITURE never borrows NAMTAR's visual language, which §3.1 enforces.

## 1.3 What must not be advertised

Straight from the brief, and every line here is a build constraint, not a
preference:

- **No footage, renders, concept art or engine tests presented as representative
  quality.** None exist. Every image slot on both surfaces ships as a designed
  placeholder with a file number and an honest stamp (§3.3).
- **No cut or deferred features.** Corrupt cops, player lawyers and judges,
  heists, hacking, a federal-investigator role, and districts beyond the launch
  four or five are post-1.0 or unbuilt. They do not appear in any string.
- **No "coming soon", no countdown, no date.** `SCOPE.md` §6 has no dated gates
  past the slice. A fabricated date would be the one false sentence on the page.
- **No console, no cross-platform, no free-to-play.** PC and Steam, Early
  Access, premium. `GDD.md` §16 is explicit and the F2P rejection is a
  *differentiator worth publishing*, not a limitation to hide.
- **No price.** §16 targets $25–35, which is a real decision, but a number
  published in pre-production gets quoted at a storefront two years later and
  the studio gains nothing today by printing it. **Publish the model, withhold
  the number.** See Appendix B.
- **No gangster imagery, ever.** Including stock photography, including a
  placeholder, including a link preview card.
- **No other game is named on either surface.** Not a competitor, not an
  inspiration, not as flattery. This plan's competitive analysis is internal
  and stays internal; what reaches the page is the *appetite* a reader arrived
  with, never the product that gave it to them. Three reasons, and the third is
  the one that matters: naming somebody else's trademark inside our own
  advertising is avoidable legal exposure; any comparison dates the moment that
  game ships an update or dies; and a page that defines itself against a list
  of other games is, at the bottom of it, telling the reader what to go and
  play instead. The appetite is the durable thing, so the page describes that.

## 1.4 What the page can honestly say about status

Everything below is true today and stays true regardless of what the slice
teaches:

- Ki-Ra Studios, Unreal Engine 5, dedicated server, first-person.
- Persistent online multiplayer, 32 players per shard at launch, architected
  toward more.
- PC and Steam, Early Access, premium. Never free-to-play.
- Four to five densely built districts at launch, not eight to twelve.
- Pre-production complete. Vertical slice planned, not built.
- No wipes, no seasons, ever.
- Self-hosting and a dedicated-server binary are first-class, not an
  afterthought. *(This is the strongest single line available to the FiveM
  audience and the brief under-weights it   see Appendix B.)*

# PART II   How it is advertised

## 2.1 The strategy in one line

**Sell the position to the two adjacent audiences who are already unhappy, and
let the depth pull the rest.**

Hero copy speaks to FiveM and Rust first   the moat line and the loss-design
message   because they have the highest intent and the shortest distance to
travel. The detective loop and the Chronicle do the work of converting the
EVE and investigation-game audiences once they have scrolled.

## 2.2 The hook, in priority order

1. *Every crime leaves evidence. Every detective is another player.*   the back
   half of the Steam one-liner, and the eight words that do the most work.
2. *Law enforcement is a profession here, with its own progression, played by
   other people.*   the moat, stated flat.
3. *Heat is what the city suspects. Proof is what a detective can carry into an
   interview room.*   the system, drawn.
4. *Loss is asset loss, never time loss.*   the objection-handler for the Rust
   audience, and the reason a skeptic keeps reading.

## 2.3 Tone, and the one thing the copy must not do

The brief: *dry, specific, competent   closer to "run something real, and do not
get caught" than "become a kingpin." Headlines should read like a case file or a
business plan, not a movie poster tagline.*

This is already the site's voice. The failure mode to watch for is the opposite
of the usual one: satcorp.io's Concierge register is playful in places   "the
safe houses", "arrange passage", "this evening's programme"   and playfulness
applied to *this* subject curdles into exactly the crime-glamour the brief bans.

**The rule for this page: the site's furniture may be theatrical; the claims
about the game may not be.** Stamps, file numbers and the dossier grammar stay.
Winking about crime does not. Nothing on the page should imply that the studio
thinks any of this is cool.

## 2.4 The sequence

| Phase | What ships | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **Now** | The `/kira` section and the `/forfeiture` page, copy-only, placeholder frames, forwarding list open | This plan approved |
| **On first art** | Real frames drop into the existing placeholder slots. No layout work | Art that passes the art bible's own review checklist (`ART-BIBLE.md` §12) |
| **On first playable** | A single unedited capture, captioned as what it is | Slice kill-criteria passed (`SLICE-PLAN.md`) |
| **On first shard** | The Chronicle section becomes a link to a real, rendered shard history | A shard with history worth reading |
| **On store listing** | Steam wishlist CTA replaces the forwarding list as the primary action | A Steam page that exists |

The page is built so that every one of those is a data edit, not a rebuild.

## 2.5 What success looks like

Ninety days after publication, none of these are pageview numbers:

- **Forwarding addresses**, with the free-text "why you are here" field filled
  in. That field is the recruiting channel and the qualitative signal at once.
- **Inbound from people who want to build it.** A persistent-sim crime MMO is a
  hard hire; this page is the cheapest filter the studio has.
- **One creator or outlet explaining the evidence system in their own words.**
  Press and creators before paid, always. A third party explaining chain of
  custody is credibility no ad buy can purchase.
- **Zero corrections.** Nothing on the page had to be walked back, because
  nothing on it was a promise.

## 2.6 The advertiser's anti-goal   held as doctrine, not printed as a manifesto

**Built, then cut. Read §3.2 and the development record before reinstating it.**

The brief's "what not to advertise" list inverts cleanly into commitments, and
the instinct to print them as a set is strong   every line is a decision
already taken, so publishing costs the studio nothing today while making it
expensive to be cynical later. The build proved the instinct wrong twice over:
a section headed *what we are not selling you* spends credibility before the
reader has finished learning what the game is, and every line in it was already
true somewhere else on the page as a **fact** rather than a promise.

The list still governs the page. It is a checklist for whoever writes the copy,
not a block the reader scrolls past:

- No footage of a game that does not exist.
- No release date until there is a real one.
- Nothing for sale that touches money, assets, heat or capacity. Ever.
- No free-to-play, and no subscription at launch.
- No seasons. No wipes. Nothing ever gets erased.

An audience burned by three crime MMOs recognises the shape of this list   but
it recognises it faster in an empty contact sheet stamped NOTHING SHOT YET, a
standing ladder with `[REDACTED]` where the dates would be, and a dossier fact
that reads *Premium. No free-to-play, no subscription.* Demonstrated beats
declared, and the page now demonstrates all five.

# PART III   Implementation

## 3.0 The placement decision

The brief asks for both surfaces and both are correct:

**A section on `/kira`**, because that page is the studio's slate and a studio
with three announced titles that shows two is under-selling itself. It sits
after the second feature, in the screening room's own running order   *feature
presentation* (NAMTAR), *the second feature* (RELENTLESS), *the third feature*
(FORFEITURE). It is an **announcement**: the hook, the one visual, the two
professions, and a link.

**A full page at `/forfeiture`**, as its own establishment with its own tab,
because that is where a reader who has decided to care can be asked for
something   and because a section on somebody else's page cannot honestly run a
forwarding list.

## 3.1 Theme fidelity   the non-negotiables

Both surfaces are BLK / RED / BONE, the Concierge typography, the dossier
grammar, the grain and vignette. The reconciliation between the site's palette
doctrine and the game's art bible is the accent, and it is the one genuinely new
decision in this build.

**The accent is institutional blue-grey, `#5a7d94`.**

Three reasons, in order:

1. **It is the game's own reserved colour.** `ART-BIBLE.md` §3.1 rations colour
   to meaning and reserves *police blue and amber* for law-enforcement presence.
   The page's entire argument is that law enforcement is real here, so the page
   spends the law's colour on itself. Nothing else on the site is making that
   claim, so nothing else should carry it.
2. **It is unlike anything on the site.** ANU brass, Ki-Ra and RELENTLESS teal,
   NAMTAR orange, PULSE hot red, KYRAX near-white. A cold, desaturated
   institutional blue reads as its own establishment on arrival.
3. **The obvious choice was wrong.** Sodium-vapour amber is the game's signature
   night light and would have been the instinctive pick   and it lands within a
   few degrees of NAMTAR's `#d97e2f`, which is precisely the collision §1.2
   exists to prevent.

The art bible's rule that police blue is *never used decoratively* governs the
game world, not the marketing surface. It is worth recording that the exception
was taken deliberately.

Grain sits at `0.07`, heavier than Ki-Ra's `0.04`. The brief asks for
photographic and worn; the texture layer is where the site says that without a
single image.

## 3.3 Where the constraint becomes the art direction

There is no art. The site's placeholder discipline (`BUILD-PLAN.md` §12) turns
that into a designed statement rather than an apology: registration marks,
hatching, a rubber stamp and a file number.

The stamps here are written to be read as honesty, not as tease:

| Slot | Stamp |
|---|---|
| Environment and district frames | `NOTHING SHOT YET` |
| Anything that would be a trailer | `NO FOOTAGE EXISTS` |

Not `COMING SOON`, and not `AWAITING CLEARANCE`   the first is a promise and the
second is RELENTLESS's legal situation, not this one's.

This section is now where the page makes its no-fake-footage commitment, and it
makes it by **being** the case rather than by announcing it. Six frames, all
empty, stamped with what is actually true. That is the argument the cut covenant
was trying to make in a sentence, and it is stronger here because a reader does
not have to take anybody's word for it.

Frame labels are drawn from `ART-BIBLE.md` §5's district kits, so that the empty
frames still advertise the world: the Flats at 03:00, Dockside under high-mast
sodium, Kestrel Row in the evening, an evidence bag, an interview room, a
seizure notice.

## 3.5 The one bespoke component: `ProofLine`

The brief's second pillar   *evidence is physical, not a meter*   is the hardest
thing on the page to convey in prose, and the most valuable. Every competitor
has a wanted-level meter. The argument is that this game has two quantities that
behave differently, and the difference between them is the whole detective loop.

The drawing:

- **Heat** (bone, a smooth curve): spikes the moment a crime happens, then
  decays on its own. It is what the city suspects.
- **Proof** (accent, a stepped line): flat at zero until somebody physically
  collects something. It rises in discrete steps, because evidence is discrete.
  **It can fall**   a swept scene, a broken chain of custody   and that fall is
  the single most important shape on the page.

Smooth versus stepped is the argument, not decoration. Heat is atmosphere; proof
is a物 you can carry. The one thing no competitor's wanted meter can draw is a
line that goes *down* because somebody picked up a shell casing and then lost
the paperwork.

Same engineering discipline as `CycleClock`: an SVG polyline derived from a
table of points rather than a hand-tuned path string, `vectorEffect` holding the
stroke weight even, GSAP drawing it once on scroll, no canvas, no three.js, and
a full `aria-label` so the argument survives with images off. Deliberately not a
second WebGL scene   the performance budget is enforced and an SVG says this
better anyway, at 360px, in a screenshot and in print.

Caption, and it is also the `aria-label`   one string, so the drawing reads the
same to a screen reader as it does to an eye:

> Two lines over one case. Heat spikes the moment a crime happens and then
> decays on its own, whatever anybody does. Proof stays at nothing until
> evidence is physically recovered, rises in steps as it is collected, falls
> when the chain of custody breaks, and only then climbs high enough to charge.

**There is no pull-quote underneath it, and there must not be one.** The build
shipped with one and it was cut: the drawing states the argument, the caption
explains it, and a third telling in display italic was the writer enjoying
himself. If this section ever feels like it needs a closing line, the thing it
actually needs is a better drawing.

> · Investigation

**The moat**
> Law enforcement is a profession here, with its own progression, played by
> other people.
>
> Consequence is enforced by the simulation, not by volunteers with moderator
> powers. There is no application, no whitelist and no appeal thread — and the
> city still works when nobody is moderating it. The other side of the law is a
> career with a ladder of its own, not a scripted NPC on a patrol route.

**The four pillars**
> **I — The city runs while you are logged off.** The economy, the rival
> organisations and the pressure the police are under all keep moving. Your
> warehouse can be raided at 4am — but the raid takes real time, makes noise,
> and can never zero you out.
>
> **II — Evidence exists in the world and has to be collected.** Nothing
> accumulates on its own. Somebody has to find it, bag it and log it, and the
> chain of custody can break along the way. You can sweep a scene clean before
> the first unit arrives.
>
> **III — There are no wipes and no seasons.** Every war, every notorious player
> and every district held since year one stays in the shard's history, and stays
> there permanently.
>
> **IV — Losing costs you property, and at most forty-five minutes.** A
> confiscated van, the cash inside it, the warehouse it was parked at. The
> sentence itself is playable — contacts worth making, contraband worth moving,
> work that shortens it — and your organisation can bail you, buy you out, or
> come and get you.

**The covenant**   *not page copy. It was built as a section and cut (§2.6).
Kept here as the checklist it became, for whoever writes the next line:*
> No footage of a game that does not exist. · No release date until there is a
> real one. · Nothing for sale that touches money, assets, heat or capacity. ·
> No free-to-play, and no subscription at launch. · No seasons, no wipes,
> nothing ever erased.

**The Chronicle**
> The wars, the market crashes, the districts held since year one, the longest
> uncaught criminal career — rendered as a public record that no reset ever
> takes away. It writes itself: every input already exists in the ledger, the
> case files and the territory log.
>
> Designed and specified, not built. No shard is running yet, so there is no
> history to show. It is also the reason the game will never wipe.

**Field notes**
> Development notes when there is something worth sending, and not on a schedule
> invented to look busy. No countdown, no pre-order, and nothing to install for
> a long while yet.

## Appendix B   Open decisions for the studio

1. **The price band.** `GDD.md` §16 targets $25–35. This plan withholds it (§1.3).
   Publishing it early wins nothing today and can only be walked back. **If the
   studio disagrees, it is one string.**
2. **Self-hosting deserves more weight than the brief gives it.** §16 makes the
   dedicated-server binary, configuration layer and scripting API first-class
   and calls community servers "our marketing budget and our long tail." For the
   FiveM audience — the brief's own beachhead — *you can run your own* may be a
   stronger conversion line than anything about evidence. It is on the page in
   the apparatus block. It may deserve to be a pillar.
3. **The Discord.** Both surfaces link the studio's existing room, shared with
   RELENTLESS and the ARK clusters. If FORFEITURE is going to draw the FiveM
   audience in volume, a shared room will not hold. The brief's own rule applies:
   *do not offer a Discord nobody is going to moderate.*
4. **`forfeiture.game` is available at registration price** (`NAMING.md` §2.3).
   Nothing in this plan needs it — `satcorp.io/forfeiture` is the right home
   while the studio is the story — but it is cheap and it will not stay
   available forever.
5. **The Chronicle section ships at half strength** and stays that way until a
   shard has history. It is built so that the day it does, the section becomes a
   link and nothing else changes.

## Deviations from the plan, and why

**1. The `/kira` section renders in Ki-Ra's teal, not FORFEITURE's blue.**
Accent is set by `[data-division]` on the route group, so a section living on
`/kira` inherits the studio's accent. Overriding it for one section would break
the establishment system, which is the site's central conceit, for a benefit
that lasts until the reader clicks through. The section is distinguished
instead by taking the tinted band the second feature gives up, so the change of
subject is visible before a word of it is read. `/forfeiture` is where the blue
arrives, and arriving somewhere new is what the accent is for.

**2. The covenant was built seventh, and then cut entirely.** Worth keeping
both halves of this on the record. The temptation during the build was to lead
with it   it read as the best copy on the page   and it was placed seventh
instead, because leading with what the studio will *not* do, before a reader
knows what the game is, reads as defensive rather than confident. The studio
then made the same argument one step further and removed it. Correct: seventh
was better than first, and absent is better than seventh. See entry 5.

**3. No other game is named on either surface.** Added after the first build,
at the studio's instruction, and it is the right call   §1.3 now carries it as
a published constraint and `forfeiture.ts` as its sixth copy rule.

The block it threatens is the audience section, which was built on five
competitor names. It survived intact because the names were doing less work
than they appeared to: each card's *want* and *answer* were already written
about the appetite rather than the product, so only the five labels changed  
**Crime roleplay · Production chains · Persistent PvP · Player-driven economies
· Investigation**. A reader who arrived from any of those recognises their own
card in a word, without the page telling them what else to go and play. The
fantasy line lost its comparison and kept its humility.

Worth recording plainly: this is the stronger page. The competitive read
belongs in Part I of this document, where the studio argues with itself. It was
never load-bearing on the page, and the appetite outlives every product that
could have been named beside it.

**4. The brief's tagline was cut.** *"The rules are in the code, not in the
Discord"* shipped in the first build in three places   the hero, the pitch
panel, and the close of the `/kira` section   and came out at the studio's
instruction on the same day.

It is the right cut and §1.1 ① now argues for it rather than around it. The
line fails the brief's own voice test: *headlines should read like a case file
or a business plan, not a movie poster tagline.* It is a movie poster tagline.
Worse, for a studio whose entire published position is "hold us to this", a
slogan is the one kind of sentence nobody can be held to.

What replaced it is the same claim with the joke removed   *law enforcement is
a profession here, with its own progression, played by other people*   plus a
body paragraph that keeps every load-bearing specific the slogan was gesturing
at: no application, no whitelist, no appeal thread, and a city that works when
nobody is moderating it. Duller on the page, stronger in an argument.

The `/kira` restatement did not come back. Without a slogan to land, a sentence
under the two-professions table was only saying in prose what the table had
just proved row by row. The table carries it alone now.

**5. A corn pass, and it cost a section.** Five pieces of copy came out or were
rewritten at the studio's instruction, and they were all the same construction:
a two-beat antithesis, or a personification, built for the sound of it.

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| *Evidence is physical. It is not a meter.* | **How a case gets made.** |
| *Heat is what the city suspects. Proof is what a detective can carry into an interview room. Only one of them can take your warehouse.* | Cut. The drawing and its caption already said it twice |
| *Every shard writes itself down.* | **The Chronicle.** |
| *It is not written yet, because there is no shard yet…* | *Designed and specified, not built. No shard is running yet, so there is no history to show. It is also the reason the game will never wipe.* |
| *What we are not selling you*   the whole section | Cut. See §2.6 |

The lesson is worth writing down because it will recur: **this page's subject
supplies all the drama it needs, and prose that adds more reads as a writer
enjoying himself.** A shell casing, a chain of custody and a forty-five-minute
sentence are already interesting. The copy's only job is to be accurate about
them, and every line cut here was a line that had stopped doing that job and
started performing.

The covenant is the real loss and it is a small one. Four of its five
commitments were already on the page as facts a reader has to walk past
anyway   the empty contact sheet, the `[REDACTED]` ladder, pillar III, and the
dossier's model row, which now reads *Premium. No free-to-play, no
subscription.* The fifth, *nothing for sale that touches money, assets, heat or
capacity*, is genuinely homeless and is flagged in `forfeiture.ts` where the
section used to be. It is the strongest anti-pay-to-win line the design
documents authorise and the GDD asks for it to be published early, so it should
come back   as one plain sentence beside the standing ladder, never as a boxed
manifesto.

**6. The four pillar titles were rewritten, and the rule behind it is worth
keeping.** *Evidence is physical, not a meter* survived the first corn pass
because it sits in the pillars list rather than in a heading, and it is the
marketing brief's own name for that pillar. It came out in a second pass, and
the other three came with it, because pillar titles read as a set and fixing
one of four makes the other three look worse.

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| *A city that runs without you* | **The city runs while you are logged off** |
| *Evidence is physical, not a meter* | **Evidence exists in the world and has to be collected** |
| *Nothing ever wipes* | **There are no wipes and no seasons** |
| *Losing does not waste your evening* | **Losing costs you property, and at most forty-five minutes** |

The heading above them went too   *Four systems, not four adjectives* was the
same construction, standing directly over four examples of it.

The rule, now written into `forfeiture.ts` above the list: **an "X, not Y" is a
comparison to something unnamed, not a description of anything.** A reader
cannot hold a studio to a contrast. They can hold it to *jail is capped at
forty-five minutes*. On a page whose entire proposition is "hold us to this",
that difference is the whole argument, and it is why the plainer titles are also
the stronger ones rather than merely the less embarrassing ones.

Two second-order edits fell out of it, and both are the kind of thing that gets
missed. Plain titles took the vocabulary the notes used to open on   *while you
are logged off*, *no seasons*, *exists in the world*   so each note was rewritten
to add specifics instead of echoing the line above it. Pillar II's note then
duplicated the opening sentence of the evidence section almost verbatim, and was
rewritten again.

**7. Four fixes rather than the two §3.4 anticipated.** The registry alias
list also gained an entry for `relentless`, which had never had one: that
division has been unreachable from KYRAX's Ask the Registry since it was added.
One line, in the file already being edited.
